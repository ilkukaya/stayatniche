Morocco has been pulling travelers across the Strait of Gibraltar for millennia, and it earns every journey. Mediterranean coast to the north, Atlantic to the west, Sahara to the south, and the High Atlas — nearly 4,200 metres — cutting diagonally through the middle. Each zone has its own climate, culture, and distinct character. Few countries offer this much variety within a single itinerary.


Morocco's four imperial cities each served as the country's capital at different points and each retains a distinct personality. Fez is the most ancient and most disorienting: its medina, Fez el-Bali, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site that functions today essentially as it did in the 14th century. The tanneries where leather is still processed using medieval methods — saffron, poppy, indigo, pigeon dung in round stone vats — are the city's most photographed sight. But the medina rewards those who spend days rather than hours within it. Hire a licensed guide for your first morning; the streets genuinely disorient even experienced navigators.

Marrakech runs at a higher velocity. The Djemaa el-Fna square at dusk, with snake charmers, storytellers, and mobile food stalls, is one of the world's great urban spectacles. The riads hidden behind the medina's blank exterior walls offer the counter-experience: private courtyards with central fountains, orange trees, and the silence of thick earthen walls.


The riad — the courtyard house turned boutique hotel — is one of the world's great accommodation formats. The finest riads in Fez and Marrakech are works of craft as much as hospitality: hand-carved plasterwork (zellij tilework, muqarnas ceilings), cedar-wood mashrabiya screens, hand-painted geometric ceilings. The concept of the inward-looking house, blank exterior and elaborate interior, creates privacy and calm that feels increasingly rare. Book the best ones months ahead for spring and autumn travel — they fill early.


The Erg Chebbi dunes near Merzouga rise to 150 metres and extend for 22 kilometres. The standard experience — camel trek to a luxury desert camp at dusk, proper beds and private bathrooms in Berber-style tents, stars over the dune crest, sunrise light on the sand — is theatrical and deliberate and genuinely beautiful.


The High Atlas is a different Morocco entirely. Villages cling to valley walls above terraced barley and almond fields; mule trains are the primary transport above the road line. The Toubkal Circuit (two to three days) ascends North Africa's highest peak — achievable for most fit hikers — with summit panoramas over a sea of lower ranges extending toward the Sahara. [Cave hotels](/categories/cave-hotels) and kasbah lodges built from pisé (rammed earth) in the Draa Valley and Skoura palm groves are among the most atmospheric accommodation in the country.


Direct flights reach Marrakech and Casablanca from London, Paris, Madrid, and major European hubs. CTM buses are comfortable, reliable, and cheap between cities. The train network connects the northern cities well. In the south — Draa Valley, Sahara routes — a driver and 4WD is both more convenient and more atmospheric than self-driving.
