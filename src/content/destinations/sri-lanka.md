Sri Lanka earns its reputation as one of Asia's most rewarding short-haul destinations. In a roughly 65,000-square-kilometre island, smaller than Ireland, you can progress from 5th-century rock fortress ruins to Victorian-era tea estates to leopard-spotted savannah to coral reef within a single week. The island's compactness is its great advantage; its cultural layering, Sinhalese Buddhist, Tamil Hindu, Dutch colonial, British imperial, and a vibrant contemporary hospitality scene, is its great depth.


Sigiriya Rock Fortress rises sheer from the flat jungle plain of Sri Lanka's Cultural Triangle, a volcanic monolith of 200 metres, topped with the remains of a 5th-century palace built by King Kashyapa. The ascent involves climbing iron staircases bolted to the rock face, passing famous frescoes of heavenly maidens painted directly onto the sheer cliff wall, and crossing a bridge through a giant stone lion's paw entrance (the lion, now largely eroded, gave Sigiriya, 'Lion Rock': its name). The view from the summit, across the jungle to the distant hills, is extraordinary.

The Cultural Triangle formed by Sigiriya, Dambulla, Polonnaruwa, and Anuradhapura contains the most concentrated collection of ancient Buddhist sites in Asia outside the Pagan plains of Myanmar. Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka's first great capital, contains stupa and monastic ruins spanning 1,500 years of continuous occupation.


The central highlands around Kandy, Nuwara Eliya, and Ella represent a landscape that British colonists immediately recognised as resembling their homeland, and proceeded to transform with tea. The Ceylon tea industry, established in the 1870s after a coffee blight destroyed the previous plantation crop, created the emerald-green terraced hills that define Sri Lanka's most photographed landscapes today.

The train journey from Kandy to Ella through this landscape is one of Asia's genuinely great rail experiences. The line climbs through tea estates, tunnels through mountains, crosses the famous Nine Arch Bridge at Demodara (best photographed from the hillside above), and descends into the cooler air of the hill country. Tea estate bungalows, converted from the colonial-era residences of British plantation superintendents, are Sri Lanka's most characterful accommodation category: stone fireplaces, brass fixtures, lawns of clipped grass at 1,500-metre elevation, with valley views over a sea of tea.


Yala National Park, in the southeast corner of the island, holds the world's highest recorded density of wild leopards, an estimated one per 10 square kilometres. The leopards here are comparatively habituated to vehicles and are seen with a regularity unusual for a notoriously secretive species. The park also supports sloth bears, elephants, crocodiles, and over 200 bird species. Early morning game drives in the dry season (April to October) offer the best encounter rates.

Offshore, Sri Lanka's deep-water submarine canyon system creates the conditions for blue whale aggregations between December and April, the world's largest animals feeding within 10 kilometres of the coast near Mirissa. The whale watching season here is among the most accessible in the world: a 30-minute boat ride from the beach puts you in the vicinity of animals routinely exceeding 25 metres in length.

The south coast's surf and colonial architecture (Galle Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage walled city of Dutch colonial buildings) provide a further distinct layer. Boutique hotels within Galle Fort's narrow streets, converted from Dutch merchant houses with roof terraces overlooking the Indian Ocean, are some of Sri Lanka's most atmospheric and sought-after accommodation.


Sri Lanka's boutique accommodation landscape has been transformed by a generation of creative hoteliers. [Treehouse hotels](/categories/treehouse-hotels) in the jungle around Sigiriya, converted tea estate bungalows in the highlands, minimalist design lodges perched on Ella's hillside with valley-facing infinity pools, and tented camps at the Kaudulla elephant gathering ground (where 200+ elephants congregate in October and November) represent an accommodation scene of genuine sophistication that punches well above Sri Lanka's price point.
