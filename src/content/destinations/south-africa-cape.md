South Africa's Cape region delivers an almost unfair concentration of exceptional experiences. Within a 200-kilometre radius of Cape Town sit some of the most dramatic coastal scenery in the southern hemisphere, two of the world's great wine valleys, a remarkable diversity of marine wildlife, and a city whose setting, between two oceans, beneath a mountain with a flat top that functions as a natural weather station, has been called the most beautiful in the world. It is a claim that is difficult to argue with from the summit of Table Mountain on a clear day.


Cape Town occupies a narrow peninsula where the Atlantic and Indian Oceans collide: literally, at the Cape of Good Hope, where the Benguela and Agulhas currents meet. The city's geography is unusual: Table Mountain (1,086m) rises almost directly from the city bowl, providing a backdrop of extraordinary drama visible from everywhere. The cable car to the summit is a cliché that earns its reputation; the Platteklip Gorge hiking trail offers a 90-minute alternative with the city spread below as you climb.

The V&A Waterfront, Camps Bay beach strip, and Bo-Kaap neighbourhood (with its brightly coloured Cape Malay houses) are the tourist focal points, but the city rewards exploration of its less-visited quarters: the Old Biscuit Mill market in Woodstock on Saturday mornings, the Constantia wine estates within city limits, and the long beaches of Muizenberg and St James on the False Bay side, where Victorian bathing boxes line the promenade.


The Stellenbosch, Franschhoek, and Paarl wine valleys lie less than an hour from Cape Town and together represent one of the southern hemisphere's greatest wine regions. The Cape's unique terroir, ancient granite and sandstone soils, Mediterranean climate, ocean-cooled mountain air, produces Chenin Blanc and Syrah of exceptional quality alongside the Cabernet-dominant blends for which the Cape has long been known.

Franschhoek is the most architecturally beautiful of the wine towns: a single main street of Cape Dutch facades, with estates climbing the valley walls behind it and mountain views at every turn. Several estates here have converted historic manor houses into exceptional boutique hotels: stone-floored rooms under thatch, private plunge pools among the vines, and breakfasts served on terraces as the mountain mist burns off.


The Cape's marine environment is among the world's richest, fed by the cold, nutrient-dense Benguela Current. Boulders Beach near Simon's Town supports a colony of around 3,000 African penguins, easily the most accessible penguin encounter in the southern hemisphere, with boardwalks allowing close approach to birds that have become remarkably accustomed to human presence.

At Hermanus, on the Overberg coast two hours east of Cape Town, Southern Right Whales arrive from their Antarctic feeding grounds to calve in Walker Bay between July and November. The cliffs above the bay allow viewing from a height of 30 metres, close enough to see the whales' callosities (the rough white patches on their heads) and hear the exhale from their blowholes. It is the world's best land-based whale watching.


The Cape region's accommodation landscape ranges from celebrated wine estate hotels in Franschhoek and Stellenbosch to clifftop boutique properties overlooking the Atlantic at Camps Bay and beyond. The [safari lodges](/categories/safari-lodges) at private game reserves within day-trip distance of Cape Town, Aquila and others in the Karoo, allow visitors to combine city and wildlife experiences within a single trip. Treehouses and forest lodges along the Garden Route, particularly in the Tsitsikamma National Park, offer immersion in South Africa's coastal forest ecosystem.


East of Cape Town, the Garden Route stretches for 300 kilometres of forest, lagoon, and rocky coast to the dramatic Tsitsikamma section. The [treehouse hotels](/categories/treehouse-hotels) and forest lodges in this area are among South Africa's most inventive accommodation, elevated timber structures in old-growth yellowwood forest, with the sound of the Indian Ocean audible through the tree canopy. The Otter Trail (five-day coastal hike) and the Storms River Gorge suspension bridge are the region's great outdoor experiences.
