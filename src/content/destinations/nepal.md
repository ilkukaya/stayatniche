Nepal is among the world's most geographically extreme countries. In fewer than 200 kilometres of north-south distance, the landscape descends from the world's highest point (Everest at 8,849m) through the high Himalayan arc, across the middle hills of the Mahabharat range, to the subtropical Terai plains at barely 70 metres above sea level. This compression of altitude zones generates extraordinary ecological diversity — from arctic tundra and glacial moraine to subtropical jungle within the borders of a single small country. For travellers, it means that a single Nepal journey can encompass rhinoceros in lowland jungle, Buddhist monastery perched above the cloud line, and summit views of a quarter of the world's 8,000-metre peaks.

## Everest Base Camp — The Classic Trek

The trek to Everest Base Camp (5,364m) from Lukla airport is Nepal's most famous journey — 130 kilometres return through Sherpa villages, rhododendron forest, and high-altitude yak pasture to the moraine camp at the foot of the Khumbu Icefall. The route passes through Namche Bazaar (the Sherpa capital, with acclimatisation days best spent here), Tengboche Monastery (the highest monastery in the world, with the Everest massif visible directly behind it), and the final approach through glacial terrain to Base Camp itself.

The scale of the mountains above Namche is something that photographs consistently fail to convey. The Khumbu valley is narrow and steep; the peaks above — Ama Dablam, Lhotse, Nuptse, and eventually the pyramid summit of Everest — are so close and so large that the human brain has difficulty processing the scale at first sight. Trekkers regularly report standing in silence for extended periods simply attempting to absorb what they are seeing.

## The Annapurna Circuit

The Annapurna Circuit, encircling the Annapurna massif in a 200-kilometre loop, is widely considered the world's finest mountain trek in terms of landscape variety and cultural richness. The route crosses the Thorong La pass (5,416m), descending into the rain-shadow plateau of Mustang where Buddhist culture has been preserved in relative isolation from both Nepal's modernisation and Tibet's political upheaval. Ancient walled cities, cave monasteries, and a landscape of wind-sculpted red and ochre desert that could be on another planet await those who reach the Mustang side of the pass.

## Kathmandu — Complexity and Beauty

Kathmandu's Durbar Squares — there are three, at Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur — are the surviving remnants of medieval Newari civilization, a culture that dominated the valley for over a thousand years and produced an architectural and artistic tradition of extraordinary sophistication. The pagoda temple form, invented here and later exported across Southeast Asia, reaches its most elaborate expression in the woodcarved struts, gilded roofs, and intricate metalwork of the valley's great temples.

Boudhanath Stupa, east of the old city, is one of the world's largest and most important Buddhist stupas — a pilgrimage site for Tibetan Buddhists throughout the region. The circumambulation path (kora) around the stupa's base is lined with prayer wheel galleries, butter lamp shrines, and shops selling thankas, singing bowls, and Buddhist texts. Walking the kora at dusk as butter lamps are lit and monks begin evening prayers is one of the most affecting experiences in Asian travel.

## Mountain Lodge Accommodation

Nepal's tea house culture — simple but warming lodges along the trekking routes — has evolved over decades into a network of mountain accommodation that ranges from dormitory sleeping to private en-suite rooms with valley views at high altitude. The best tea houses in the Everest region offer hot showers (solar-powered), yak cheese and Sherpa stew menus, and the sociability of a shared dining room where trekkers from 20 countries compare day's stories around a central stove.

The Chitwan lowlands offer a completely different accommodation dimension: wildlife lodges on the edge of Nepal's first national park (one-horned rhino, Bengal tiger, sloth bear, Gangetic river dolphin) that function much like African [safari lodges](/categories/safari-lodges) in their design and approach — guided elephant grass walks at dawn, jeep drives through the sal forest, and canoe trips on the Rapti River where gharial crocodiles sun on the muddy banks.
