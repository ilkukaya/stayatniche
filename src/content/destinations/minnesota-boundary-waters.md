The Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness does not accommodate every kind of traveler. There are no roads into the interior, no motorboats on most lakes, no electricity, and no cell service. What there is: a million acres of boreal lake country where the only way forward is by paddle and portage, where the loon's call across a still lake at dusk is the defining sound, and where the Milky Way appears with a clarity that most Americans never experience in their own country. For travelers who understand what wilderness actually means, the Boundary Waters is one of the greatest destinations in North America.

## Why the Boundary Waters Are Perfect for Niche Travel

The BWCAW offers a type of accommodation that does not appear on most travel platforms: the backcountry campsite. Every designated campsite in the wilderness is a steel fire grate, a pit toilet, and a flat area for tents, and the site is yours alone for the night, in a landscape of extraordinary beauty that you have earned by paddling and portaging to reach it. For travelers who want roofs and beds, the Gunflint Trail lodges (Bearskin Lodge, Gunflint Lodge, Seagull Outfitters) are wilderness properties in the traditional Minnesota resort tradition, log cabins on private resort lakes with full outfitting services and a dock. Ely's resorts on adjacent lakes provide comfortable base camp accommodation with full canoe trip planning services.

## The Canoe Routes

The BWCAW contains over 1,500 miles of designated canoe routes threading through 1,200 lakes and ponds. Entry points are numbered 1 through 116 along the wilderness boundary; each opens a different system of lakes and portages. Entry Point 1 near Ely, on Moose Lake, is the busiest, and for good reason. The Moose Lake chain provides quick access to excellent interior fishing and camping within a few hours' paddling. For quieter routes, the Gunflint Trail's entry points (32–47) access less-traveled lake systems in the wilderness's eastern section. The most ambitious routes, the Frost Lake Loop, the Little Indian Sioux South to Big Moose Lake, require a week or more and repay every day invested.

## Ely: The Outfitting Capital

Ely (population 3,400) is the gateway community that has organized itself entirely around wilderness access. Outfitters like Piragis Northwoods Company, Boundary Waters Outfitters, and Canadian Border Outfitters provide complete canoe trip packages, canoe, paddle, life jackets, tent, sleeping bags, stove, food, bear canisters, maps, and trip planning, for travelers with no camping equipment of their own. The International Wolf Center and the North American Bear Center operate research and education facilities open to visitors. The Ely food culture is modest by urban standards but the community's relationship to wilderness is genuine and the outfitters' knowledge is encyclopedic.

## Wildlife and Ecology

The BWCAW is home to gray wolves, moose, black bears, beavers, river otters, osprey, bald eagles, and the common loon, Minnesota's state bird and the sound most strongly associated with the lake country. The wolf population here is among the healthiest in the lower 48 states; hearing a pack howl across the water at night from a backcountry campsite is one of North America's most powerful wildlife encounters. Moose, the largest members of the deer family, wade into lakes to feed on aquatic vegetation; seeing one standing chest-deep in a boreal lake at dawn is an image that does not leave you.

## Winter Wilderness

The BWCAW in January is an entirely different destination. The lakes freeze solid to 2–3 feet in a typical winter, creating a highway system across the interior. Dog-sled tours operate from Ely outfitters across frozen lake surfaces and through boreal forest. Nordic skiing on the Gunflint Trail's groomed system and in the wilderness itself, where skiers travel between camps on the frozen lakes, produces one of the most hands-on winter wilderness experiences in North America. The aurora borealis is visible here on clear nights from September through March, particularly during periods of high solar activity.
