The Great Barrier Reef is 2,300 kilometres long, covers 344,400 square kilometres, and contains more species of fish, coral, and marine invertebrates than most people's imaginations can comfortably accommodate. It is also, at its most accessible points near the major ports, heavily trafficked by day-trip boats whose passengers are back in Cairns hotels by late afternoon. The live-aboard expedition exists to take you somewhere different entirely.

Departing from Cairns marina in the late afternoon, the vessel runs overnight to reach outer reef locations that day-trip boats cannot access within a practical operating window. By the time you surface from your first dive the following morning, you are in genuinely remote reef, bommies rising from a white sand bottom, coral walls dropping 30 metres to blue water below, and a fish count per dive that experienced divers rate among the highest they have encountered anywhere in the Indo-Pacific.

The variety across two days is remarkable. Shallow bommie tops, where parrotfish methodically crunch living coral and Maori wrasse the size of Labradors cruise past with territorial authority. Deeper walls where sea fans filter the current and schools of barracuda form slow, turning cylinders in the blue. Night dives where sleeping turtles resting on coral heads are revealed by torchlight, and Spanish dancer nudibranchs the size of dinner plates perform their strange undulating dance across the sand.

For non-certified divers, Discover Scuba sessions with qualified instructors are conducted in the sheltered conditions of the vessel's back deck before entry, allowing snorkellers and absolute beginners to access the reef under direct supervision.

Meals on board are hearty and plentiful, the crew are experienced, and the sound of the reef at anchor, gentle current, occasional fish splash, the creak of the mooring line, makes for surprisingly good sleeping between dives.

**Best time to visit:** Year-round, though June to October offers the best visibility (often 20-30 metres) and most comfortable temperatures for multiple dives per day.
