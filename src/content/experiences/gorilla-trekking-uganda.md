There are 1,063 mountain gorillas left in the world. They live in a narrow band of Afromontane forest spanning Uganda, Rwanda, and the Democratic Republic of Congo, and they exist nowhere else. Trekking to meet them is not like a game drive or a safari boat cruise, it is an active, often physically demanding journey through ancient forest that ends with one of the most emotionally overwhelming encounters in all of wildlife travel.

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest is aptly named. The vegetation here is genuinely dense, a tangle of giant tree ferns, climbing vines, centuries-old Podocarpus trees, and volcanic soil that turns slippery after rain. Trek times to reach a habituated gorilla family range from forty minutes to seven hours depending on where the group has moved overnight, and your ranger team of trackers, guides, and security scouts follows the gorillas' trail from the previous day. The effort of the approach makes the arrival all the more powerful.

When you find the family, and you will, UWA rangers have a near-perfect record, the protocol is to maintain seven metres of distance, though the gorillas themselves set the actual terms. Juveniles tumble through the undergrowth, indifferent to the watching humans. Mothers nurse infants with the unhurried confidence of absolute safety. And the silverback, the patriarch who may weigh 200 kilograms and whose presence fills a clearing the way a force field fills a room, typically reclines, watching your group with measured, intelligent calm.

You have one hour. It is never enough. Most trekkers report that the hour passes in what feels like minutes, and that no other wildlife encounter they have had before or since quite compares.

**Permit booking:** Permits are limited and in high demand. Book three to six months in advance for peak season (June-September, December-February). The $700 permit fee directly funds gorilla conservation and community development programmes.
