Angkor Wat was built between 1113 and 1150 AD, covers 200 hectares, and is the largest religious monument ever constructed by human hands. The Western Causeway that approaches the main temple is oriented deliberately to frame the sunrise over the central tower complex on the equinox, a feature that reveals both the sophistication of Khmer astronomy and the temple's function as a cosmic map as much as a place of worship. Standing on that causeway in darkness as the sky slowly greys behind the towers, surrounded by other quiet observers and the first birds of the Cambodian morning, is one of those experiences that stays with you.

The tuk-tuk picks you up from your hotel at 5 a.m., a time that sounds punishing until you understand that it is the single most important decision of the entire day. By 7:30 a.m., tour buses from the resort hotels begin depositing passengers at the west entrance in their hundreds. Before that hour, Angkor Wat belongs to the early risers.

Your guide uses the morning light to explain the kilometre-long bas-relief galleries that encircle the inner sanctuary, epic carved narratives of the Mahabharata, the Battle of Kurukshetra, and the Hindu creation story of the Churning of the Ocean of Milk, all rendered in stone with a narrative complexity and artistic quality that continues to astonish scholars. Each panel rewards careful attention; your guide knows which figures to look for and what they mean.

From Angkor Wat, the day moves through the Angkor Thom complex, a city of temples within a city, centred on the Bayon with its 216 enormous serene faces, and then to Ta Prohm, where strangler fig trees have colonised the stones with spectacular theatrical effect, their roots draping over lintels and splitting towers in a display of nature's patient, irresistible power.

**Practical tips:** The three-day Angkor pass represents excellent value and allows a deeper exploration. Dress codes apply: knees and shoulders must be covered at all Angkor temples.
