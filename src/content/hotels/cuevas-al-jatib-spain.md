In the white hill villages of the Serranía de Ronda, where Andalusia folds into a landscape of dramatic limestone gorges and ancient footpaths, the tradition of living in caves carved from the soft local rock stretches back through the Moorish period and beyond. Cuevas Al Jatib, the name draws on the Arabic heritage of the village of Benalauría, has made this tradition its central proposition, restoring a cluster of historic cave dwellings into some of the most characterful accommodation in southern Spain.

The village of Benalauría sits at over 700 metres above sea level in the Genal Valley, a landscape of chestnut forests, terraced orchards, and whitewashed cortijos that has changed little since the Reconquista reshaped Andalusia in the fifteenth century. The cuevas are carved directly into the limestone cliff at the village's edge, their whitewashed facades giving no hint of the cool, vaulted spaces within, rooms where the rock walls maintain a natural temperature of around 18°C throughout the year, warm in winter and refreshingly cool in the heat of summer.

Each cave suite has been restored with a light and respectful touch. The original rockwork is preserved and celebrated rather than plastered over: rough limestone arches frame doorways, curved ceilings rise above the beds, and the particular quality of light that filters into cave spaces, soft, diffused, timeless, creates an atmosphere of deep calm that is difficult to achieve in conventionally built rooms. Handwoven textiles, traditional Andalusian ceramics, and furniture crafted from local chestnut wood furnish each space with appropriate simplicity.

Breakfasts are served on the terrace overlooking the Genal Valley: fresh orange juice from the nearby citrus groves, local mountain cheeses, cured meats from the region's celebrated jamón production, honey from Serranía hives, and bread baked in a wood oven. The view down the valley, chestnut forest dropping away to the blue shadow of the Sierra Bermeja, is as fine as any in Andalusia.

The surrounding landscape offers excellent walking: the Genal Valley hiking network connects Benalauría to a dozen other white villages, and the historic city of Ronda, with its extraordinary clifftop setting and eighteenth-century bullring, is just thirty kilometres away. For those drawn to the deep texture of Andalusian culture, this cave hotel in the mountains represents one of the most authentic and quietly moving experiences the region offers.
