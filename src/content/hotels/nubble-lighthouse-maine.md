Cape Neddick Light, built in 1879 on a small granite island called The Nubble, separated from the York Beach mainland by a narrow tidal channel, is by most measures the most photographed lighthouse in America. The white tower, the red-roofed keeper's house, the American flag, and the rocks below have appeared on postcards, calendars, paintings, and photographs in their hundreds of thousands. NASA included an image of it on the Voyager spacecraft's Golden Record as an example of human civilisation — an unlikely form of validation, but a real one.

Staying on the mainland opposite the lighthouse is not a single experience but a constellation of them, offered by the inn and guesthouse properties that line the York Beach shoreline with views toward the Nubble. The best rooms face directly west toward the island, and the views they offer change dramatically through the day, from the cool blue morning light that makes the white tower luminous against the Atlantic sky, through the warm mid-afternoon gold, to the extraordinary colour that floods the horizon at sunset behind the lighthouse on clear summer evenings.

York Beach is a New England coastal resort of the classic variety, with a character that mixes authentic Maine fishing-town heritage with the comfortable seaside pleasures that have drawn summer visitors for over a century. The seafood here is the real article: lobster landed from working boats, served at weatherboard shacks a few steps from the sand, at prices that reflect proximity to the source. Long Sands Beach is a mile of Atlantic-facing sand that handles the swell produced by summer south-westerlies with respectable consistency, drawing surfers as well as swimmers and sunbathers.

The surrounding southern Maine coast, Ogunquit with its Marginal Way cliff path, Kennebunkport with its grand shingle-style summer cottages, the Rachel Carson National Wildlife Refuge, extends the possibilities for exploration well beyond the lighthouse itself. This is a region of extraordinary natural beauty and considerable culinary interest, with a restaurant culture that extends well beyond the excellent seafood to include some of the finest farm-to-table dining in New England.

Visit in September or October to combine the lighthouse photography with the extraordinary autumn foliage of the Maine coast, when the crowds thin, the temperatures are perfect for walking, and the light takes on a quality that summer can never quite match.
