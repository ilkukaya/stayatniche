OFF Paris Seine is moored on the quay between the fifth and thirteenth arrondissements, with the buttresses of Notre-Dame visible upstream and the towers of the Bibliothèque Nationale visible downstream. The view it offers — Paris from the river level, looking outward at the city's banks — is genuinely different from what the grand hotels of the Right Bank or the boutique addresses of Saint-Germain provide. It's a perspective that most Parisians have only from a bateau-mouche, and here it's stationary.

The hotel was designed by the Paris architectural practice and inaugurated in 2017. The barge structure is modern and deliberately architectural, not a converted historic vessel but a purpose-built floating hotel that takes the clean industrial vocabulary of the Seine's working watercraft and refines it into something that holds its own against the extraordinary urban theatre of its surroundings. The exterior, in weathered steel and timber, is modest and contextual; the interiors, designed with the particular French talent for making small spaces feel generous, make the most of the river proximity through large panoramic windows in every room.

Staying at OFF Paris Seine produces an entirely different experience of the city's rhythms. The Seine is a working river, barges pass, tourist boats circle with their spotlights in the evening, rowing crews emerge at dawn, and from a river room these processes feel intimate and immediate rather than observed from a distance. The reflection of Paris's illuminated bridges and monuments in the water at night is among the most beautiful things the city offers, and the floating hotel delivers it as a private view from the bed.

The rooftop deck is the hotel's social centrepiece. On warm evenings, Paris's young creative classes, architects, designers, filmmakers, gather here for drinks as the sun sets behind the Left Bank and the last bateaux-mouches of the day pass below. The restaurant on the lower deck serves a competent contemporary French menu with an emphasis on seasonal produce and natural wines. Breakfast is a proper French affair: croissants, tartines, fresh-pressed juice, and strong café au lait served as the morning mist lifts off the river and the first joggers appear on the quay.

Location is, of course, this hotel's supreme advantage. The fourth and fifth arrondissements, Île de la Cité, the Marais, the Latin Quarter, are within comfortable walking distance; the thirteenth arrondissement's new architecture and restaurant scene is immediately adjacent. Paris is outside the door, and the door, unusually, happens to be on a river.
