Cappadocia is one of those landscapes that defies rational description and rewards surrender to the merely sensory. The volcanic tufa of the Göreme Valley, soft, cream-coloured, riddled with the caves and chambers carved by early Christian communities two millennia ago, has been shaped by millennia of erosion into formations that bear no resemblance to anything in the ordinary geological vocabulary: mushroom-capped fairy chimneys, sinuous valleys, cliff faces honeycombed with Byzantine frescoes. To sleep within this rock, in a cave suite carved from the same material as the surrounding landscape, is to experience Cappadocia at its most elemental.

Cappadocia Cave Suites occupies a historic building in the heart of Göreme, tunnelling back into the hillside through a series of vaulted rooms and carved corridors that have been transformed into some of the most carefully finished cave accommodation in the region. The suites vary in character, some occupy original Byzantine cave spaces with smoothed tufa walls and barrel-vaulted ceilings that curve overhead like frozen waves; others are cut from the living rock with more angular precision, finished with traditional Anatolian tiles and kilim cushions that bring warmth to the monochrome stone.

The property's terrace is among the finest vantage points in Göreme. At dawn, and dawn is a specific and important event in Cappadocia, the valley below fills with hot air balloons at almost the same moment the first light hits the fairy chimneys. The sight of sixty or eighty balloons rising silently from the valley floor in the pink light of a Turkish morning, drifting over formations that look like the landscape of a dream, is something that guests describe, without apparent exaggeration, as one of the most beautiful things they have ever witnessed. The hotel's terrace puts guests directly in the centre of this spectacle, and the adjacent rooftop bar, serving Turkish tea and fresh pastries, means no one needs to watch it from a cold street corner.

Below ground, a hammam carved from the living tufa offers traditional steam treatments and massage; above ground, the hotel's staff navigate the considerable complexity of Cappadocia's attractions, underground cities, frescoed churches, pottery villages, and sunset horse rides over the Rose Valley, with the knowledge of people who have grown up within this landscape. Breakfasts are substantial and local: Cappadocian pastries, sheep's cheese, olives, fresh vegetables, and honey from the region's volcanic hillside hives. It is, in every respect, a complete immersion in one of the world's most extraordinary landscapes.
