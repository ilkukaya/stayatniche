At the very southwestern tip of Australia, where the continent finally runs out of land and two great oceans, the Indian and the Southern, meet in a perpetual collision of swells and spray, stands Cape Leeuwin Lighthouse. Built in 1895 from local limestone and rising 39 metres above the headland, it is Australia's tallest mainland lighthouse and one of its most significant maritime heritage sites. To stay in the restored keeper's cottages at its base is to occupy one of the most geographically and atmospherically charged addresses on the continent.

The lighthouse has guided mariners around this notoriously treacherous cape for over 130 years. The waters below are a graveyard of ships that failed to make the passage, and the lighthouse's beam, still operational, sweeps across both ocean surfaces each night as it has done since Federation. Staying here, you feel the full weight of that maritime history in the limestone walls, the iron-framed windows, and the way the wind moves around the buildings with a kind of oceanic insistence.

The self-contained heritage cottages have been carefully restored to preserve their historical character while providing comfortable modern amenities. Thick limestone walls provide natural insulation against the coastal elements, and the interiors balance period authenticity with practical comfort. Each cottage has a full kitchen, necessary given the remote location, and private outdoor areas where you can watch the ocean do its restless work on the headland below.

Access to the lighthouse tower is included, and the guided climb to the lantern room provides views that justify the journey alone: to the north, the karri forests of Leeuwin-Naturaliste National Park roll toward the horizon; to the west and south, the ocean extends uninterrupted to Antarctica. At the right time of year, humpback whales pass within extraordinary proximity of the headland on their annual migration, and the cape's elevated position makes it one of the finest land-based whale watching spots in Australia.

The surrounding national park offers walking trails through coastal heath and wildflowers, and the famous Margaret River wine region lies only an hour's drive north, making this an ideal base for combining wilderness solitude with exceptional food and wine. Augusta township provides basic provisions, while the cape itself provides everything you came for: wind, ocean, history, and the rare sensation of standing at the edge of a continent with nothing between you and the Antarctic Circle.

Book well in advance for the June-to-September whale season. This is one of Australia's genuinely rare coastal experiences, and availability is limited.
