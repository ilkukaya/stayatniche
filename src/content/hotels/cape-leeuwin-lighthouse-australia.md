Cape Leeuwin is the southwestern tip of Australia, the point where the Indian and Southern Oceans collide in a perpetual intersection of swells and spray. The lighthouse here has been operational since 1895, 39 metres of local limestone, Australia's tallest mainland lighthouse, its beam still sweeping both ocean surfaces each night. The waters below are a historic maritime graveyard. Staying in the restored keeper's cottages at its base puts you in one of the most geographically emphatic addresses on the continent.

The limestone walls of the heritage cottages insulate against the wind that moves around these buildings with oceanic persistence. The interiors balance period character with functional comfort: full kitchens, private outdoor areas facing the headland. The guided lighthouse tower climb is included, and the views from the lantern room earn the journey, karri forests rolling north toward the horizon, the ocean extending uninterrupted to Antarctica in every other direction.

June through September, humpback whales pass within extraordinary proximity of the headland on their annual migration. The cape's elevated position makes it one of the finest land-based whale watching spots in Australia. Book early for these months; availability is limited and the whale season draws people who know what they're looking for.

Margaret River wine country is an hour north, one of Australia's finest food and wine regions, genuinely worth the day trip. Augusta township provides basic provisions. The national park's walking trails through coastal heath and wildflower meadows are right on the doorstep. Everything else at Cape Leeuwin is wind, ocean, history, and the specific sensation of standing at the edge of a continent with nothing between you and the Antarctic Circle. That turns out to be enough.
