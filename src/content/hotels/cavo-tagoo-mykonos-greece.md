Mykonos has many luxury hotels, but Cavo Tagoo occupies a category largely of its own. Perched on a volcanic cliff above the old harbour of Mykonos Town, with the whitewashed maze of the Chora spreading below and the Aegean stretching to the horizon beyond, it was one of the first hotels in Greece to understand that Cycladic architecture, the pure white geometry, the cave-like rooms carved from living rock, the absence of unnecessary ornament, was not a vernacular limitation to be overcome but a design language of exceptional power.

The original property opened in 1980 and was designed by architect Nikos Daskalantonakis, who made the radical decision to let the cliff itself shape the architecture. Rooms were cut into the volcanic rock, their walls following the irregular geometries of the stone rather than imposing rectangular order. The result is a series of spaces that feel genuinely cave-like, in the best possible sense, with the particular quality of light, temperature, and acoustic intimacy that only rooms carved from natural rock can achieve. Subsequent renovations have updated finishes and introduced contemporary amenities while preserving the fundamental architectural character that made the property remarkable in the first place.

The swimming pool, or rather, pools, are among the most photographed in the Mediterranean. The main infinity pool appears to float at the cliff's edge, its water merging visually with the Aegean several hundred metres below. The famous champagne pool, a smaller elevated basin where guests are served Moët & Chandon while overlooking the harbour, has become one of Greece's most recognisable luxury images, appearing in countless magazines and social media posts without ever quite capturing the particular sensation of sitting there in the late afternoon light, with the Cycladic wind cooling the champagne in the glass.

The suites draw on a vocabulary of natural materials, rough limestone, bleached timber, raw linen, and the furniture is minimal in the manner of genuinely confident design. Cave suites have their original rock formations as walls; garden suites open directly to private terraces with plunge pools. The restaurant, occupying a terrace with unrestricted sea views, serves contemporary Greek cuisine with the quality of produce that the island's connections to Athens and the wider Mediterranean make possible. Breakfasts at Cavo Tagoo, served at whatever hour guests choose, with fresh pastries, Greek yogurt, and honey from Mykonos hives, are a particular pleasure before the Aegean sun reaches its full heat.
