Belgium may not be the first country that springs to mind when dreaming of extraordinary outdoor accommodation, but the Bubble Hotel in the Antwerp Province woodlands makes a quietly compelling case for reassessment. Set within a private nature reserve near the village of Pulderbos, these transparent dome accommodations bring the concept of sleeping under the stars to the low countries with real style and attention to detail.

Each bubble occupies its own discreet forest clearing, sufficiently separated from neighbouring domes to ensure complete privacy, a thoughtful detail that elevates the experience from novelty to genuine retreat. The transparent shell of each dome is engineered to remain clear through Belgium's temperamental weather, and the climate control system maintains a comfortable interior temperature throughout the year, whether the night outside is frosty or mild.

The star of the show, naturally, is the sky. Belgium's rural Antwerp Province sits far enough from major light pollution sources that on clear nights, which are more frequent here than the country's reputation might suggest, the Milky Way makes a genuine appearance. The king-sized beds are positioned precisely beneath the apex of each dome, so guests wake to either stars or soft dawn light filtering through the canopy.

Beyond the sleeping experience, the property excels in thoughtful details: a private hot tub beside each dome, fire pits stocked with seasoned wood, and the morning arrival of a breakfast basket laden with local charcuterie, Ardennes cheese, fresh bread, and Belgian preserves. The surrounding woodland trails are excellent for walking, and the medieval city of Antwerp is less than an hour away by car: close enough for a cultural day trip, far enough to feel genuinely remote.

The Bubble Hotel appeals to couples seeking a romantic short break with substance. It is unusual enough to feel special, practical enough to be genuinely comfortable, and rooted in a landscape that has its own understated beauty: rolling Belgian woodland, birdsong, and the particular quiet of a northern European forest in the hours before dawn.
