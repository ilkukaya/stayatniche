The approach to Alila Jabal Akhdar prepares you, but not quite enough. The drive from Nizwa into the Al Hajar Mountains climbs through date-palm oases giving way to raw limestone, hairpin bends opening onto canyon vistas, old stone villages on ridge lines, Omani goats negotiating terrain that seems to defy gravity. At 2,000 metres the plateau opens, the temperature drops ten degrees, and the resort appears: a sweeping complex of stone and whitewash that looks as though it grew from the cliff edge rather than being placed there.

Below it is Wadi Ghul, which the tourism materials call the Grand Canyon of Arabia. Both canyons are too singular for easy comparison, but the scale is genuinely comparable — a kilometre of vertical drop to the wadi floor. Standing at the clifftop terrace and looking down into that void doesn't photograph well, which is to say, no photograph manages to communicate what the actual view does to your sense of scale.

The architecture uses local rose-coloured stone quarried from the same mountains, and the stepped terraces and angular profiles echo the falaj irrigation systems and terraced villages that have worked these hillsides for centuries. Most rooms and suites have private plunge pools positioned at the cliff edge. The first view from yours produces a sharp intake of breath regardless of how many other hotel pools you've stood beside.

The resort sits 10°C cooler than coastal Oman — a fact that makes it one of the Gulf's most appealing summer escapes when the cities are punishing. Winter nights drop close to freezing, which suits the fireplace-equipped interiors well.

The Overcliff Spa uses frankincense, damask rose water from the valley's famous rose gardens, and hammam traditions that feel genuinely local rather than generically "Arabian." The via ferrata routes on the cliff face are excellent, the guided hikes lead into villages where honey is still harvested from traditional hives set into rock crevices, and the night sky at this altitude, with near-zero light pollution, is the kind of thing you'll still be talking about when you get home.
