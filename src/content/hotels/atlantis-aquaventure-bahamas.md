Atlantis Paradise Island is not a hotel in any conventional sense: it is a destination that happens to have rooms. The resort complex, spread across the northern end of Nassau's Paradise Island and connected to the Bahamian capital by a bridge, encompasses eleven hotels, more than 40 restaurants, a casino, the Aquaventure waterpark, and most relevantly, an 11-million litre marine habitat system called The Dig that constitutes one of the world's largest marine wildlife displays integrated into a resort environment.

The marine habitat is the element that elevates Atlantis beyond its competitors in the Caribbean mega-resort category. The Dig is presented as an "underwater excavation" of the mythical city of Atlantis, and its narrative conceit, sunken Atlantean ruins repurposed as aquarium tanks, is elaborate and consistent. Corridors and walkways pass through transparent tunnels surrounded by water; enormous tanks house species including sawfish, stingrays, sand tiger sharks, and tropical fish in quantities that reflect the scale of the overall system. Guests staying in the Coral Towers or Royal Towers can book suites with floor-to-ceiling viewing windows directly onto the major habitats, falling asleep to the movements of sharks and rays in illuminated water, an experience that sits somewhere between the Manta Resort's underwater room and a conventional oceanarium, and is wholly particular to Atlantis.

The Aquaventure waterpark deserves its reputation as the best in the Caribbean. The 141-acre complex includes more than 20 slides, including the famous Leap of Faith, which sends riders down an near-vertical drop through a shark-filled lagoon, a mile-long river ride, 11 pools, and a private beach of considerable quality. For families, the combination of waterpark and marine habitat makes Atlantis one of the most complete family resorts in the Atlantic basin.

The resort's scale means dining options are genuinely varied: Nobu, Virgil's BBQ, Café Martinique, and a dozen other restaurants provide options ranging from Japanese fine dining to Caribbean casual. The casino, the largest in the Caribbean, and the nightlife complex ensure that adult guests are not restricted to the aquatic entertainment, and the resort's proximity to Nassau means the cultural and historical dimensions of the Bahamas are accessible within twenty minutes.
