The Peruvian Amazon around Puerto Maldonado — specifically the Tambopata National Reserve and its surrounding protected area — is one of the highest-biodiversity ecosystems ever recorded. A single hectare of forest here may contain more species of ant than the entirety of the British Isles contains of all insects; a single day's birding can yield 200 species; a night walk reveals a density of amphibian, reptile, and arthropod life that makes the darkness seem inhabited in ways that no temperate landscape can match. To sleep in the canopy of this forest, at the level where the light penetrates and the most active layer of the ecosystem concentrates its greatest abundance, is an experience of extraordinary biological richness.

Inkaterra's canopy treehouse at the Reserva Amazónica is reached by suspension bridge from the main lodge and climbs to a platform 30 metres above the forest floor — the level at which the primary canopy closes overhead and the understory below begins to thin. At this height, the perspective on the forest reverses completely: rather than looking up through the canopy toward the light, guests look down into a green depth, and the crowns of the giant Brazil nut trees and cecropia spread out at eye level in a horizontal landscape of extraordinary complexity.

The treehouse platform is furnished for sleeping and observation: comfortable beds, mosquito netting, and a railing from which the forest can be watched at dawn. Dawn is the event for which guests wake early and stand silently: the transition from forest night to forest day is one of the great acoustic experiences of natural travel, as thousands of birds and amphibians shift vocal registers simultaneously, and the light moves through shades of grey, green, and gold. Macaws, toucans, and tanagers pass at eye level or above; howler monkeys announce their territories from adjacent trees; and on clear mornings the mist lifts through the canopy in layers that are nothing short of cinematic.

The broader Inkaterra programme — clay lick excursions to watch hundreds of macaws and parrots consuming mineral-rich clay at dawn, oxbow lake tours for giant river otter sightings, night caiman spotting, and guided walks focused on medicinal plants, insect life, or amphibians — is managed by a team of naturalists who hold university-level biology qualifications and bring a depth of ecological knowledge that transforms a wildlife holiday into a genuine education. It is among the most seriously managed ecotourism operations in the Amazon basin.
