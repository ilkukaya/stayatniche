It begins before you even enter a room. The approach to Amangiri, across the flat, ochre immensity of the Utah desert, with the layered strata of the Grand Staircase rising in geological time to the north, prepares you, perhaps, but does not quite ready you for what you find when the driveway reveals the resort in full: a long, low complex of poured concrete and desert stone that doesn't impose itself on the landscape so much as inhabit it, like a thing that grew here slowly over millennia.

The central geological feature around which architect Marwan Al-Sayed and his collaborators designed the entire resort is an ancient sandstone mesa, an outcropping of the same Navajo sandstone that forms the canyon walls of the surrounding Grand Staircase-Escalante. The building wraps around it with what can only be described as reverence: the main pool is built directly against the rock face, the spa treatment rooms hover over it, and every sightline in the public spaces has been calibrated to return attention to the mesa's sculptural presence.

Rooms and suites are essays in restraint. Raw concrete walls, warm sand-coloured textiles, beds oriented toward floor-to-ceiling windows that frame specific sections of the canyon panorama, every element has been considered with a precision that goes beyond interior design into something closer to landscape architecture. The private plunge pools that accompany each suite are positioned to reflect the colours of the sky at specific times of day, and in the canyon country of southern Utah, those colours, from the electric blue of a desert morning to the saffron-and-crimson theatrical production of a Utah sunset, are among the most extraordinary available anywhere on the planet.

The Aman Spa extends over five thousand square metres within the rock, its treatment rooms incorporating the natural geological formations of the site into their design. The floating pool, built directly over a crack in the mesa, creates an experience of profound spatial disorientation: you float in warm water, surrounded by ancient desert, with nothing visible below you but rock.

Beyond the resort, the American Southwest offers an embarrassment of geological drama. Guides take guests by helicopter to formations accessible no other way, by kayak across the glassy surface of Lake Powell, and on foot into slot canyons that compress the sky to a sliver of blue between walls of undulating sandstone. Amangiri has the rare distinction of making these extraordinary landscapes feel both accessible and intimate, always returning guests to the silence and space of the desert, which is, in the end, the best thing about the place.
