Jules' Undersea Lodge occupies a category of one. There is no other property in the world that requires guests to put on scuba equipment, submerge in a lagoon, and enter their accommodation through a moon pool in its floor, and yet the lodge has been doing exactly this in the mangrove lagoon of Key Largo Undersea Park since 1986, making it not merely the world's first but the world's longest-operating underwater hotel.

The structure itself began life as La Chalupa research laboratory, a scientific habitat used by researchers studying the Florida Keys marine environment in the 1970s. Converted to its current purpose in 1986, it rests on the lagoon floor at a depth of approximately six metres, anchored to a weight-bearing structure and supplied with breathable air pumped from the surface. The interior, pressurised to prevent water entry through the open-bottomed entrance moon pool, comprises a main living area with a kitchen, dining table, and the famous porthole windows, plus two separate private sleeping cabins. The windows are circular and approximately 45 centimetres in diameter, positioned at eye level when seated, and through them the lagoon's inhabitants are constantly visible: sergeant majors and damselfish, the occasional barracuda, grouper moving slowly through the structure's shadow, and the various species that have colonised the lodge's exterior since it settled on the bottom.

The access requirement is central to the experience and to the character of the guest community it produces. Guests who are not certified divers must complete a three-hour orientation course, the minimum required to achieve the brief dive, and the activity of entering and exiting the lodge multiple times during a stay is itself a significant part of the pleasure. Waking at 3am in an underwater room, pulling on a wetsuit, and slipping through the moon pool into a lagoon lit only by bioluminescent plankton and the dim ambient light from the surface is an experience that very few people on Earth have had, and that remains among the more extreme accommodation experiences available anywhere.

The practicalities are managed with good humour. Meals are delivered by diving couriers from the surface kitchen, the underwater pizza delivery is one of the lodge's most celebrated features. Showers are hot, the sleeping cabins are snug but comfortable, and the sound of small fish investigating the porthole glass is a soporific accompaniment to what is, for most guests, a genuinely unusual and memorable night's sleep.
