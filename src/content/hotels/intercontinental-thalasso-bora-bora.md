The InterContinental Bora Bora Resort and Thalasso Spa occupies the most exposed and dramatic position of any overwater resort on the island: it sits on the outer reef itself, on Motu Piti Aau, a small coral island at the southern end of Bora Bora's barrier reef. From here, the lagoon spreads northward in a panorama that encompasses the full drama of Bora Bora's silhouette, Mount Otemanu and Mount Pahia rising from the main island while the lagoon shimmers in colours that range from near-turquoise to near-sapphire depending on the angle of the light.

The resort's most significant innovation is practical rather than architectural: the Deep Ocean Water Air Conditioning system draws cold seawater from 900 metres below the Pacific surface through a pipe 3,000 metres long, and uses it to cool the resort's buildings without conventional refrigeration. The system reduces the resort's cooling energy consumption by approximately 90 percent and eliminates the refrigerants required by conventional air conditioning, a genuinely meaningful contribution to the sustainability of a resort that would otherwise impose a substantial energy burden on a remote coral island. It is also, from a guest perspective, entirely invisible: the rooms are cool and comfortable in the tropical heat, the mechanism is silent, and the only evidence of the technology is the warmth of the water returned to the ocean surface after cooling.

The Deep Ocean Spa is built on the reef itself, with treatment rooms suspended over the lagoon and thalassotherapy pools using deep-drawn seawater that is measurably richer in minerals than surface water. The treatment menu combines traditional Polynesian massage techniques, hot stone, coconut oil, monoi flower rituals, with contemporary wellness approaches, and the setting, over-water, with the lagoon visible through glass floor panels below and the Pacific horizon visible through open walls, gives the spa a quality of tranquillity that is genuinely difficult to replicate.

The overwater villas follow the standard Bora Bora template, thatched roofs, generous decks, glass floor panels, lagoon steps, but with finishes and proportions that reflect the resort's position at the premium end of French Polynesian hospitality. The reef fish directly below the villas include several species not commonly seen in more sheltered lagoon positions, and the drop-off to deeper water within snorkelling distance brings larger pelagic visitors, white-tip reef sharks, eagle rays, and occasional barracuda, into regular contact with guests prepared to explore beyond the immediate shallows.
